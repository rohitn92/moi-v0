## CHAPTER - I

### History of Indian Music

The history of Indian music may be broadly classified under three heads, Viz., Hindu Period, Mohammadan Period and British Period.

1.  **Hindu Period:** From Indus Valley Civilization to the 10th Century.
2.  **Mohammadan Period:** From 11th Century to the 18th Century.
3.  **British Period:** From 19th, Century until India gained independence.


#### (1) HINDU PERIOD

The origin of Indian music has been described by various schools of thought which are mostly religious having fanciful legends of gods and goddesses without any historical authenticity. It is said in Hindu mythology that, for the first time, Brahma created the science of music. He taught this art to god Shiva. And, Shiva taught this art to goddess Saraswati. From Saraswati the art passed on to Narad, who is said to wander about on earth and in heaven. Thereafter Narada is said to have taught this art to Gandharvas (heavenly singers), Kinnars (heavenly performers on musical instruments) and to Apsaras (heavenly female dancers). In the end, Narada (not the above said heavenly Narada) and Hanuman the two Rishis of fame, spread this art to the people on this earth.

Some are of the view that god Shiva created five Ragas, viz, Bhyairava, Hindol, Megh, Deepak and Shri and the sixth Raga, named Kaishik, was created by Parvati the life-partner of god Shiva. Still others opine that music has been created by the term 'OM'. This may be true. From the above description it is clear that the origin of music is based on fanciful legend, mythology and religious dogmas which cannot be easily believed as these are not authentic and also not based on historical truths. Therefore in order to understand the actual origin and history of Indian music, we have to begin it historically from the time of "Indus Valley Civilization".

**Indus Valley Civilization (3200 B.C.):** On the basis of the exvacations made by the Archaeological Department of India the Indus Valley Civilization is considered to be older than the Vedic Civilization, which is understood to be of 3200 B.C. Music was then current and cultivated by the people in right earnest. The idol of a female dancer, in a dancing pose, and some musical instruments found in the excavacations of Harappa and Mohanjodaro give ample testimony to the fact that music was very popular during those days and the standard of music was also very high.

**Vedic Period (2500 B.C.):** The Vedic period is said to begin with the advent of the Aryans in India. The Aryans are said to have come from Central Asia. The information of the vedic period can be had from the Vedic literature, namely, Rig Veda, Yajurva Veda, Sam Veda and Atharva Veda. Rig Veda is considered to be the oldest veda which dates 2500 B.C.*

> \* In a modest computation, we should come to 2500 B. C. as the date of Rig Veda." Hindu Civilization of India by Radha Kumud Mookerji. Page 67.

Music was very popular during the Vedic period, Vocal music, Instrumental music and dance were at their highest level. The general public had a good idea of music. It was well cultivated in almost every family. In every house morning and evening prayers were performed to the accompaniment of music. Dancing was indulged in by both the sexes to the accomponiment of music from cymbal (Aghati).

All the four kinds of musical Instruments, namely, 'Tata-Vadya', 'Avanaddhya-Vadya', 'Sushir-Vadya' and 'Ghana-Vadya' were prevalent during this period.

* **'Tata-Vadya' or Chordophones:** The string Instruments which were played with the bow, plectrum and fingers, e.g., Veena, Kand-Veena, Karkari-Veena etc.

* **'Avanaddha-Vadya' or Membrophones:** The instruments that were made of hollow cylinders with parchment stretched over the openings and which were played with the fingers of both the hands, e.g. Mridanga, Dhol, Dholak etc.

* **Sushir-Vadya or Aerophones:** The Instruments which were played with the blow of the mouth, e.g., Flute, Shehnai etc.

* **Ghan-Vadya or Autophones:** The Instruments which were played with the small sticks or by percussion striking one solid body against another, e.g. Jhanj, Kartal, Manjeera etc.

The character of the artists of the Vedic period was perfectly chaste and strong. They were always prepared to sacrifice any thing for the cause of music. They were considered as members of the higher class of society.

It is said by some of our learned musicians that the present classical music is derived from "Sama-Veda". But, strictly speaking, this view does not sound plausible. Because, there is no relation of the modern classical music with that of the music of "Sama-Veda". The music that was sung during the time of "Sama-Veda" had, at first three notes, viz. Udatta, Anudatta and 'Swarit'. Udatta was the higher note, Anudatta was the lower note and Swarit was considered by some as the notes in between Udatta and Anudatta, by others as the notes higher than the notes of Udatta, and still others believed to be the notes further lower than the notes of the Anudatta. After that another set of three notes, viz., 'Rig', 'Gatha' and 'Saman' came into use. Rig was sung in one note, Gatha in two notes and Saman in three notes. Besides this there was a 'Scale of four Swaras' better known as 'Swarantar.

After this the said three notes changed into seven notes, namely, Krishta, Pratham, Dwitiya, Tritiya, Chaturtha, Mandra and Ati-Swara. These seven notes were in the descending order of pitch. According to the treatise Panini-Shiksha and Naradi-Shiksha 'Ni Ga' were produced from Udatta, 'Re Dha' from Anudatta and 'Sa Ma Pa' from Swarit. But inspite of all this the relation or the interval of sound between the said three notes is not yet known. Even the interval of sound between the even notes produced from the said three notes is also not known as yet. Therefore, it can be safely said that there does not exist any relation between the Vedic music and the modern classical music. Hence, the idea that our modern classical music is derived from the music of Sama-Veda falls to the ground.

**Ramayana and Mahabharata age:** (About 1900 B.C. and 1400 B.C. respectively.) The two Epics, Ramayana and Mahabharata, in their present literary form belong to the age of "SUTRAS" though their subject matter is much older. The three classes of works, SUTRAS, IPICS and Law-books, date roughly from 800 B.C. though their events, viz, the event of Ramayana and the Bharat war, happened much earlier i.e., in 1900 B.C. and 1400 B.C. respectively.

In Ramayana there is a mention of Ravan being a good musician, both in theory and practical. He had good musicians in his court and he himself attended the musical gatherings from time to time. Mention is also made of "Jati-gayan" and various musical instruments such as Veena, Mridang, Dumdubhi, Mudduk etc. It is said that Lava and kush, the sons of Ram, received their musical training at the 'Ashram' of sage Valmiki and they had given their performance of "Marg Sangeet" before their father Ramchandra. Thus music was very popular during the age of Ramayana.

In the same way in Mahabharata we find a mention of the theory of the consonance of notes. Besides, Sri Krishna was an expert flute-player at that time. Arjun was also a good musician who had given training in music to 'Uttara' the daughter of the Raja of 'Virat'.

**PANINI'S GRAMMAR:** (About 350 B.C.) The date of Panini is usually assumed to be about 350 B.C. but it is perhaps safer to say that he lived after, probably soon after 500 B.C. He has mentioned the condition of music during his time. He has given a description of the player on Mridanga, Maddukaa and Jharjhara and of concerts of vocalists and dancers. The usual recreation of women was singing and dancing. The seven notes were designated by their initial letters as "Sa Re Ga Ma Pa Dha Ni."

**The Rulers of Magadha:** (About 500 B.C. - 325 B.C.) During this Deriod Bimbsar, Ajata Shatru and the kings of "NAND' dynesty ruled in ndia. Vocal music singing and dancing were the recreations of women. Men too were fond of singing and dancing.

**Maurya Rulers:** (322 B.C. 185 B.C.) During this period Chandra Gupta Maurya, Bindusar Shatru and Ashoka ruled in India. Among them Chandra Gupta Maurya was a great lover of music. He heard the erformances of vocal music. Instrumental music and dance and even joined them in their performances. Dancing, singing and Instrumental music formed an important limb of all festivities. Folk-music and Folk-dance were also very popular during this period.

**Kushan Rulers:** (78 B.C.185 A.D.) Kanishka was a great ruler of is period. He loved both music and literature. He was particularly and music. Many musicians adorned his court. There lived in his court a mous musician, dramatist and philosopher named Ashwa Ghosh. He wrote a poetical book named "Buddha-Charitra". The poems given in is book were sung by both men women alike with great delight.

**Natya Shastra of Bharat:** (Third Century) Shri Bharat wrote his famous grantha entitled "Natya Shastra" in the third century A.D. A detailed description of "Natya", "Nritta" and "Nritya" is given in this grantha. There is also a vivid description of Shruti, Swara, Grama, Moorchhana, eighteen kind of Jatis (Jati Gayan) etc. He has accepted 'Shruti' as a unit of measurement for fixing his Shuddha Swaras. He believes in equality of "Shrutis" and his "Shudda Scale" is of twenty two equal "Shrutis".

**Gupta Period:** (320-495 A.D.) The Gupta empire flourished for about 200 years. During this period there was a revolutionary progress in music. Singing, dancing and playing on Instruments reached their climax. All the kings of gupta dynesty were great lovers of music. It was during this period that 'Ragas' were introduced. There was all round progress in music. And, Gupta period is rightly called as the Golden age of music.

**Samudra Gupta:** (335-375 A.D.) Samudra gupta was a famous ruler of Gupta dynesty. He was very fond of literature and music. He had great respect for poets and musicians. He was a man of exceptional personal capability and unusual varied gifts. He stands forth as a real man, a scholar, a poet, a musician and a warrior. He himself was a great composer. He had composed a number of songs and rendered them in notation. He was an expert Veena-player and none could challenge him in this. He loved to witness dramas also. Singers, dancers and players on musical instruments assembled in his Court and used to give their performances from time to time. He was so very fond of music that whenever he returned after conquering any other state he would arrange music festival all over his state as a mark of his memorable victory.

**Chandra Gupta Vikramaditya (375-413):** Chandra Gupta Vikramaditya also loved music. He too had great respect for learned poets and famous musicians. The great poet Kalidas flourished in his court. He wrote two famous dramas Meghdut and Shakuntala. Kalidas composed the songs of his dramas in 'Ragas'. For example " one song which the Nati sings in the prelude to the opening act of Abhijnan Shakuntalam is composed in Raga "Madhmadi-Sarang". Thus it is clear that during this period there was an all round progress in music and literature.

**Matang Muni (Sixth Century):** Matang Muni wrote a book named Brahaddeshi in the sixth century. In this granth we find for the first time the use of the tern Raga. "He says in introducing Raga system that as the Raga-Way was neither explained nor referred to by Bharat and others, it was upto him to explain the same in accordance with the practice then current". This shows that Raga system was already ripe and had developed well respected standards in the days of Matanga. In Brahadeshi there is a mention of other topics on music almost like that of Bharat's Natyashastra.

**Harshvardhan:** (606-647 A.D.) Harshvardhan loved both music and literature. He was a good dramatist and has written three dramas, viz, Ratnavali, Priyadarshika and Nagananda. He was also a good singer. His sister Rajshri too loved music and drama and was an adept in arranging music festivals at different places. Thus, music was greatly improved during his time.

**Rajput Period:** (650-1200 A.D.) The Rajputs could not make any substantial progress in music because most of their time was consumed in fighting. But inspite of this the Rajput rulers were fond of music and had great respect for musicians of repute who were attached to their courts. Rajput women also had great interest in music. They were educated and took part in the works of public interest. The Rajput girls were well educated in Sanskrit and took part in dancing and painting. During this period many books were written in history, politics, dramas etc. 'Bhavabhutt' a great dramatist flourished during this period. He wrote three dramas, viz., Malti-Madhava, Mahabir-Charit and Uttar-Ram-Charit in which music was prominent. But, no substantial progress could be made in the field of music proper because of the insecurity that followed due to the frequent invasious of the Muslims in the eleventh and twelfth centuries.

**Naradi-Shiksha:** (10th Century) Narada (not the heavenly Narada) wrote a book named 'Naradi-Shiksha'. In this book there is a mention of Grama-Ragas. In fact, Narada introduced for the first time 'Raga-Ragini', 'Putra Raga' and 'Putra-Badhu' system which remained invogue in the Northern-system of music for a considerable period.

**Geet-Govind of Jaideva:** (Twelfth Century) The first singer of the twelfth Century was the famous Jaideva. He was born in 'Kendula' near 'Bolpur' in Bengal. He wrote the famous book entitled "Geeta-Govinda" which abounds in the love songs of "Krishna" and "Radha". But, there is no notation of these songs. This is, indeed, a great shortcoming.


#### (2) MOHAMMADAN PERIOD

From 11th Century to the 18th Century.

**Mohammadan Period:** The Mohammadans came to India, for the first time, in the 11th Century. With the advent of Muslims began the change in the music of India. Although Muslims were not much conversant with the theory of music, but they made considerable progress in its practice. They were themselves instrumental in composing and introducing several new styles of songs and devising new forms of musical instruments.

**Pandit Sharang Deva (Thirteenth Century):** Pandit Sharang Deva (1210-1247) was a learned musician in the employ of the Raja of Devagiri in the south. He was the author of the famous grantha on music known as "Sangeet Ratna Kar" in which he has given a detailed description of Vocal music, Instrumental music and Dance of his time. He has also given a good description of 'Nad', 'Shruti', 'Swara', 'Grama', 'Moorchhana', 'Jati' etc. 'Sangeet Ratnakar' is considered to be an authoritative treatise of the ancient system of music. 'Sharang Deva' accepted 'Shruti' as a unit of measurement for fixing his shuddha Swaras and believed in equality of Shrutis.

**Alauddin Khilji (1296-1316):** Alauddin Khilji was very fond of music. He encouraged the learning of music and revolutionary changes were made in music during his region. Amir Khusro the famous musician flourished in his court.

**Hazrat Amir Khusro (1253-1325):** Amir Khusro was a famous musician, poet and minister in the court of Alauddin Khilji. He was also a good saint and his religions preceptor or 'Guru' was Hazrat Nizamuddin Aoulia. He had a deep love and respect for his religious 'Guru' and composed several forms of songs in his name, e.g. 'Qoul', 'Qalbana', 'Qouwali' etc. 'Qoul', e.q. 'Hiya Dir Dir Tala Tale Hasan Wa Nizamuddin Aoulia Deem Deem Dir Dir Dir Tale Tan Tale Tana Na Na" etc. And 'Qalbana', the 'Sthai' of which is in Arabic language and the Antara is thus: "Bal Bal Jawen Hazrat Nizamuddin Ke Darbar Gawen "Qalbana". It is said that Amir Khusro was so much grieved at the sad demise of his 'Guru' that he too himself ended his life within six months after the death of his 'Guru'.

Amir Khusro introduced the following:

(a) Some new Ragas, e.g. Yaman Kalyan, Zeelaf, Sajgiri, Sarparda, Zila, Shahana, Raat-Ki-Pooriya, Todi, Barari-Tori, Asawari, Poorvi etc.

(b) Some new kind of songs, e.g., Qoul, Qalbana, Tarana, Khayal (Qouwali-Khayal) Naqsh, Nigaar, Ghazal, Solah, Tillana etc.

(c) Some new instruments such as Sitar, Tabla and Dhol.

(d) Some new 'Tals', e.g., Khamsa, Sawari, Pahelwan, Farodast, Zanani -Sawari, Pashto, Theka-Qowwali, Aada-Choutal, Jhoomra, Jald- Trital, Sool-Fakhta etc.

About this time there was a famous singer in the court of Raja Deva Rai of Vijaya Nagar in the South, Known as Gopal Nayak. He too had introduced a few Ragas such as 'Bada-Hans-Sarang', Peelu, Viram etc. It is said that Amir Khusro was considered to be wiser than Gopal Nayak in the field of music. Legend has it that once there was a musical combat between Amir Khusro and Gopal Nayak in which the latter accepted his defeat. The story of the musical combat runs thus: Once Gopal Nayak reached the court of Alauddin Khilji and presented his performance before him. At that time Amir Khusro hid himself under the throne of the king. Next day, Amir Khusro sang the same song exactly in the same manner and in the same style that Gopal Nayak had sung,. Seeing this Gopal Nayak was simply non-plused and ultimately accepted his defeat.

About this time there was another great musician named 'Baijoo Bawara'. He was a wandering minstrel singing devotional songs and songs of philosophical nature, Gopal Nayak, Bijoo-Bawara and Amir Khusro were the three contemporary musicians of their time.

**Raga-Tarangini of Kavi (poet) Lochan:** (15th Century) Kavi Lochan wrote his grantha entitled 'Raga-Tarangini' in the middle of the 15th Century. It is an authoritative grantha of Hindustani system of music. The Swara-names and Raga-names given in this grandtha are very much similar to the present Swara-names and Raga-names of Hindustani System of music. Lochan accepted twelve Shuddha and Vikrit Swaras. He has described his Ragas in terms of the said twelve Swaras. He has classified all the Ragas of his time under twelve 'Thatas'. His Shuddha 'Thata' was the present day kaphi scale or 'Kaphi Thata'. He has also given some 'Raga-Lakshanas' which are useful even to this day.

**Sultan Husain Sharqi (1458-1480):** Sultan Husain Shirqui of Jaunpur was very fond of music. It is said that he was a great Qouwali singer next to Amir Khusro. He introduced for the first time 'Kalawanti-Khayal' singing* (Kalawanti Khayal because 'Qouwali-Khayal' had already been introduced by Amir Kusro) Besides he also introduced certain new Ragas, namely, Jounpuri, Sindh-Bhairavi, Jounpuri-Todi, Raman-Todi, Rasooli-Todi, Twelve kinds of 'Shyam', (Goud-Shyam, Malhar Shyam, Vasant-Shyam, Poorvi-Shyam etc.) and Sindura. In fact, Sultan Husain Shirqi was a great 'Nayak' of his time.

**Bhakti Movement (Fifteenth Century):** During this period Bhakti movement began in Northern India, Punjab and Bengal, which was promulgated in the public through music. The chief exponents of this movement were Kabir, Nanak and Chaitanya who composed a fairly large number of 'Bhajans' which are current even to this day. The Bhajans which Kabir (1440-1518) composed were sung in Northern India. The Bhajans composed by Nanak (1469-1538) were sung in the Punjab and those composed by Chaitanya (1486-1527) were sung in Bengal. Thus music also became popular through these 'Bhajans' or devotional songs. These Bhajans led to the improvement of Bhajan-Sahitya (Bhajan-Literature) which gave further impetus to Bhajan-Sangeet. In fact, the greatest impetus that the Bhakti movement gave was to the popularisation of music and the formation of literature of devotional songs. For example, Kabir composed a very large number of Bhajans which are popular even at the present day. He was followed by Nanak and Chaitanya who gave a strong impetus to the vocal literature of the religions they respectively represented and preached.

**Raja Man Singh Tomar (1486-1516):** Raja Man Tornar was a great lover of music. He is supposed to have started the Dhrupad style of singing.* He was an expert Dhrupad singer. He composed a number of Dhrupads and encouraged Dhrupad singing. He was a great composer of songs and his compositions are current even to this day. He loved "Sankeerna Ragas'. He introduced some new Ragas and named them after his beloved queen 'Gujrani Rani', Then known as Gujari, Malgujari, Mangal Gujari. ** He wrote a book on music entitled Man Kutuhal. The most famous musician at his court was Nayak Bakhshu whose name is still considered next to Tansen. There were other famous singers also in his court named Dalu, Bhagwan, Charju and Dhondu. Thus, during the time of Raja Man there was a great progress in music and Dhrupad singing was particularly improved and held in great esteem.

**Mughal King Babar (1526-1530):** Babar was a great lover of music. He himself was a good singer and a proficient composer of songs.* He had great respect for singers and rewarded expert singers and players on Instruments. He has written a book on music which is considered of a high order.** Thus, during the reign of Babar music was considerably improved.

**Mughal King Humayun (1530-1556):** Humayun too was very fond of music. He had good respect for musicians. There were singers and players on Instruments employed in his court and were better known as "Ahal-e-Murad". "He heard sweet songs and delightful music on Mondays and Wednesdays". "ELLIOT". He did not neglect music even during his troubled days and found solace in music alone. During his time SUFIS (religious gurus) and saints spread their religious teaching through music. Thus music was spread through the saints also.

The two famous musicians, named, Ramdas, resident of Lucknow and Mahapattere were employed in his court.*** Mohammad Shah Aadil' also loved music. He was a skilled musician of no mean repute. Islam Shah Adil and Mohammad Shah Adil were only the two great partons and lovers of music.

**Akbar the Great Mughal (1556-1605):** The Emperor Akbar was a fervent lover of music. He did much for its development. He patronised musicians of real merit and his court was filled with musician of repute. He paid much attention to music and was the patron of all who practised this enchanting art. According to Prof. H. Blockmann there were numerous musicians attached to his court - Hindus, Iranis, Turanis, Kashmiris both men and women. Abul-Fazal has written in his book Ain-i-Akbari that during the reign of Akbar there has not been in India a singer like Tansen for the last thousand years.** Tansen was previously employed in the court of Raja Ram Chandra of Rewa State (Baghel Khand). After the death of Tansen the members of his family became famous as Rabab-players and those of his son-in-law's family became famous as Veenkars (Player of Veena).

**Baz Bahadur of Malwa (Sixteenth Century):** Baz Bahadur was the ruler of Malwa. He was a singer of no mean repute. He too was a unique Singer and had no rival. Abul Fazal calls Baz Bahadur a Singer without rival. Rani Roopmati of the 'Harem' of Baz Bahadur was also a good singer.

There were other famous singers during the time of Akbar. Swami Haridas was also an unrivalled singer. Nayak Baiju, not the famous Baiju Bawra but Baijnath a singer and resident of Gujrat and a pupil of Swami Haridas better known as Shekh Baijoo. A story is told that "one day the Emperor filled the tank of Hakim Ali with copper coins amounting to about 20 crores of 'dam'. He then gave an interview to sheikh Baijoo, a singer with sweet voice. Next he sent for Miyan Tansen and other unequalled singer but preferred sheikh Baijoo to any other and ordered him to carry away all the money. "Al Badauni" Vol. II Sheikh Baijoo introduced a few Ragas, viz, Lankdahan, Sarang, Dhulia-Malhar etc. Hira Bai was another good singer. She too introduced one or two new Ragas, e.g., Mirabai-Ki-Malhar.; Similarly Ramdas introduced Ramdasi-Malhar and Swami Haridas introduced Raga 'Jogiya'. Thus, during Akbar's reign some changes were made in the existing Ragas and new kind of Ragas were introduced. Not only this but musicians from Gwalior, Panna, Malwa etc., would come to the court of the Emperor and give their performances. Besides musicians of other countries also happened to come to the Emperor's court. Thus Hindus and Mohammadans borrowed from one another and each community enriched the other with the precious store it possessed.

**Pundarik Vitthals (Sixteenth Century):** During this time Pundarik. Vitthal who lived in the south in Burhanpur and was in the employ of Budhan Khan of Khandesh, wrote four important books on music entitled Sadraga-Chandrodaya, Raga-Mala, Raga-Manjari and Nartan-Nirnaya. In Sadraga Chandrodaya he has described southern music. He has accepted 19 thatas and has classified all the Ragas of the South under the said Thatas. In Raga-Mala and Raga-Manjari he has described the music of the North.

About this time Soordas (Son of Baba Ram Das) also made some contribution to music. He wrote a book named 'Soor-Sagar' in which there is a praiseworthy mixture of music and poetry. Similarly the poetic work of Tulsidas is also a good mixture of music and poetry. From the above description, it is clear that during the reign of Akbar the art of music reached it noon-day splendour and it can rightly be called as the golden age of music.

**Jahangir (1605-1627):** Jahangir, like his father, was also a great lover of music. He respected musicians and gave them gifts and presents. Jahangirdad, Chhatar Khan, Parwezdad, Khurramdad, Makkhu and Hamza were the famous singers attached to his court. When he ascended the throne he organised a grand music festival in which musicians of repute took part with great interest and pleasure. This music festival continued for a number of days, which has been described by Jahangir himself in his book Tuzak-I-Jahangiri.*

> \* "From the first day of the New year till the 19th degree of Aries, which is the Roz-i-Sharf, people gave themselves upto enjoyment and gaiety. Musicians and singers came together from every sect and race. And heart alluring Hindu dancing girls, who with a glance.could ravish the heart of an angel kept the assembly in a continual state of enjoyment." Tuzak-l-Jahangiri by W.H. Low, Bibliotheca Indica' page 40.

Jahangir's wife Nurjahan also was a great lover of music. She was a good singer. Her voice was quite sweet and attractive. Whenever there was any music programme in the court both of them took part and enjoyed it full well.

**Raga Vibodh:** During the time of Jahangir Pandit Somnath, a Telugu Brahmin and resident of Rajmundry, wrote a book entitled Raga Vibodh in 1610. He has described his Ragas in terms of Janya-Janak or Thata-Raga system. He has also given a description of different kinds of 'Veenas'. There is also some description of the North Indian music.

During the time of Jahangir Pandit Damodar Mishra wrote a book on Hindustani system of music entitled Sangeet-Darpan in 1625. In this book he has personified the Ragas, i.e., he has given a pictorial description of Ragas.

**Shahjahan (1627-1658):** Shahjahan was a great lover of music. He himself was a good singer and was an accomplished sitar player. His voice was sweet and charming. He could sing songs of Urdu language. very soulfully which simply enchanted the audience of every description. **He had in his court famous musicians, vig, Jagannath, Lal Khan (Son of Tansen and son-in-law of Bilas Khan), Dirang Khan. Jagannath was given the title of Kaviraj and Lal Khan was adorned with the title of "Ocean of excellence". Both of them were weighed with silver coins and both were given Rs. 4000/- each as reward. Shahjahan was so very fond of music that at about 8.30 p.m. when he retired to the harem again, two and sometimes three hours were spent in listening to songs by women.

> ** "Shahjahan was past master of urdu songs and his performances were sweet and charming that many pure-souled "sufis" and holy men with hearts withdrawn from the world, who attended these evening assem- Bikes, lost their senses in the ecstasy produced by his singing." "Studies in Mughal India" by J. Sarkar, Page 12-13.

**Sangeet Parijat of Pt. Ahobal:** (Seventeenth Century) About this time a learned musician Pandit Ahobal wrote a book on Hindustani system of music entitled "Sangeet Parijat" in 1650. His Suhddha Thata' was the present day 'Kaphi-Thata'. He has accepted nineteen shuddha and vikrit Swaras and has described all his Ragas on the basis of the said 19 swaras. Pt. Ahobal has also located his twelve shuddha and Vikrit nodes on the stretched wire of a veence by the simple method of bisection and trisection of the length of the wire. Thus, Pt. Ahobal has done a remarkable work by locating his swaras, in a scientific manner, on the speaking wire of veena and in terms of the length of the wire.

**Pandit-Vyankatmakhi:** (Seventeenth Century) Pt. Vyankatmakhi a learned musician of the south wrote a book on the Southern System of music entitled Chaturdandi Prakshika' in 1640. He for the first time introduced seventy two Thatas mathematically from the twelve Shuddha and Vikrit Swaras. He has described the Karnataka system of music and has accepted nineteen of the seventy-two thatas alone for the description of the Ragas of the South current during his time. He too has accepted Thata-Raga System.

**Aurangzeb (1658-1707):** Aurangzeb was an orthodox musalman. He hated music. He had dismissed all the musicians employed in his court. He had stopped singing and dancing altogether. The Emperor determined to destroy them and a severe edict was issued. Raids of Police dissipated their harmonious meetings and their Instruments were burnt". 'Rulers of India Series' by Stanley Lane Poole.

Aurangzeb hated music, dance, poetry and other fine arts He wanted to destroy music altogether. He had ordered that all the musical Instruments be buried. "Make the grave deep so that neither voice nor echo may issue from their grave". Such was his aversion for music, dance and even poety. He was an orthodox Sunny Musalman and considered singing, dancing etc. as irreligious. He believed that music destroyed the character of a person. He had forbidden music all over the state.

**Hirdaya Narani Deva:** (Seventeenth century) Hirdaya Narain Deva wrote two books on music, viz., 'Hridaya-Koutuk and 'Hridaya-Prakash' in 1669. He too accepted his Shuddha That like that of the present 'Kaphi That'. He too located his Swaras on the speaking or the sounding wire of the Veena just like that of Pt. Ahobal.

About this time Pt. Bhava Bhatt, an employee of Raja Anoop Singh of Bikaner, wrote three books on Hindustani system of music named 'Anup Sangeet Vilas', 'Anup-Sangeet Ratnakar' and 'Anupankush'. All these three books are useful for Hindustani system of music.

**Mohammad Shah (1719-1747):** Mohammad Shah "Rangeele" was an ardent lover of music. The songs bearing his name 'Rangeele' are current even to this day. There were two famous singers in his court named 'Adarang' (Naubad Khan) and 'Sadarang' (Niyamat Khan). Both of them were real brothers. They composed thousands of 'Khayals' which are current even today and are frequently sung by all the musicians all over India.

**Pandit Shriniwas:** (Eighteenth Century) Pandit Shriniwas wrote a book on Hindustani System of music entitled "Raga Tatwa Vibodh" in the first half of the 18th Century. He accepted his "Shuddha Thata" as the present day "Kaphi Thata like Ahobal, he too located his "Shuddha' and 'Vikrit' Swaras on the stretched wire of the 'Veena' by the simple method of bisection and trisection of the sounding wire, and fixed his swaras in terms of the length of the wire. Pandit Shriniwas is the last of the medeival writers. His grantha is very useful for the Hindustani system of music.

**Shori Miyan:** (Eighteenth Century) During the time of Nawab Asaf-Ud-Daulah of Lucknow (1775-1797) the famous 'Nayak' and 'Qouwal' 'Ghulam Nabi Shori' alias 'Shori Miyan', son of the famous singer and 'Qouwal', 'Ghulam Rasool', introduced 'Tappa' style of singing.

> Tappa was formerly sung in very crude style by the camel-drivers of the Punjab and it was he (Shori Miyan) who modelled it into the elegance it is now sung" A treatise on the music of India' by Captain N.A. Willard, Pg. 68.

During this time the Raja of Tanjor, Tulaji Rao Bhonsle (1763-1778), paid much attention to music and wrote a book on the 'Southern system of music' entitled 'Sangeet Sar Mritam. In this book he has fully described the southern system of music faithfully following the ideas of Pandit Vyankatmakhi.

**Nawabs of Lucknow (1756-1856):** All the Nawabs of Lucknow were fond of music. Among them Nawab Wajid Ali Shah was a great patron of music. He himself was very fond of singing and dancing. He had great respect for musicians and helped them by all means. His court was adorned by famous vocalis, Instrumentalists and dancers who gave their performances from time to time. The Nawab was also fond of dramas. He himself had written a drama known as 'Indra Sabha'. In this drama the Nawab himself took part and played the role of the Raja, This drama was played for full ten days. Besides this he introduced, "Raas Mandali" in which he took part as "Kanahaiya" and the dancing girls played the role of "Gopis".

The Nawab Wajid Ali Shah had great interest in "KATHAK' dance. He was very much impressed by the enchanting dance performance of Acharya Thakur Prasad of Lucknow. Gharana (School) of 'Kathak' dance. Thakur Prasad's father was Prakashji. Prakash ji had three sons, namely Durga Prasad, Thakur Prasad and Man singh. Thakur Prasad had no son. Durga Prasad had three sons namely Binda Din, Kalika Prasad and Bhairava Prasad. Bindadin had no son. Kalika Prasad had three sons, namely, Achchhan Maharaj (Jagannath Prasad). Lachchhu Maharaj (Baij Nath Prasad) and Shambhu Maharaj (Shambhu Nath). Achchhan Maharaj's son is Birju Maharaj and the sons of Shambhu Maharaj are Ram Mohan and Krishna Mohan. Achchhan Maharaj excelled in 'CHAALS' and lightning foot work. Lachchu Maharaj enriched graceful 'LASYA' aspect of Kathak dance and Shambhu Maharaj was the 'Abhinaya-Samraat' of Kathak dance. He was indeed, second to none in this aspect. It is said that Maharaj Ishwari Prasad Misra of 'HANDIA', near Allahabad, brought to Lucknow the style Kathak dance known as "Natwari-Nritya" during the time of Nawab Asaf-ud-daula (1775-1797). Maharaj Ishwari Prasad's nephew is Prakash ji Maharaj.

'Nawab Wajid Ali Shah was so much impressed by the dance performance of Acharya Thakur Prasad of Lucknow Gharana of Kathak' dance that he accepted him as his 'Guru' (Master) of 'Kathak' dance. About this time Maharaj Bindadin the nephew of Acharya Thakur Prasad had mastered the art of dancing at the tender age of twelve. There was a performance of Kathak dance by Bindadin in the court of the Nawab Kodou Singh the famous Pakhawaj-player of DATIA State accompaniedhis performance. Bindadin surpassed Kondou Singh by dint of his mastery in the rhythm (Layakari) of his 'TATKAR'. Thus during the time of Nawab Wajid Ali Shah, Raas-Leela, Lucknow Gharana of 'Kathak', dance, Thumri, Dadra, Qouwali and Ghazal Singing were the many sided contributirons of Lucknow to music and dance.

The famous melodious Thumri in Raga Bhairavi, viz. "Babul Mora Naihar Chhooto Jaaye" is said to have been composed by Nawab Wajid Ali Shah himself in his moments of intense grief while he was exiled and being forcibly parted from his beloved Lucknow and taken to Matiya Burj (Garden Reach) in Calcutta. The Lucknow Gharana of Tabla-Vadan (Tabla Playing) is also said to have been evolved, moulded and enriched, about this time, by Khalifa Abid Husian (1857-1936). His son-in-law Khalifa Wajid Hussain and his son Khalifa Afaq Hussain were the exponents of Lucknow-Gharana of Tabla-Vadan (Lucknow-Gharana-Style of Tabla playing). Besides. Biru Misra of Varanasi, Jahangir Khan of Indore, Hira Babu and Sapan Choudhury of Calcutta and ustad Munney Khan of Lucknow are the famous exponents of Lucknow-Gharana of Tabla playing.
